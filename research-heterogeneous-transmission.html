A Theoretical and Empirical Account of Heterogeneous Monetary Transmission across Pakistan’s Large-Scale Manufacturing Industries

Author: Salman Rashid

Overview

This project extends my 2025 MS thesis, which used a Vector Autoregression to document sharply different responses to monetary policy shocks across seven of Pakistan’s large-scale manufacturing industries. This second article develops a formal theoretical framework to explain that heterogeneity, and re-estimates the empirical model using a Vector Error Correction approach.

Central contribution

The central contribution of the study is to connect industry characteristics defined ex ante with empirically observed monetary transmission.

Rather than classifying industries after observing their estimated responses, the study defines industry profiles independently using four theoretically relevant dimensions:

  1. Capital intensity
  2. Product nature
  3. External orientation
  4. Industrial linkages

These characteristics are then used to generate and assess predictions about how industries should differ in their exposure to monetary, exchange-rate, financing, and demand conditions.

This approach allows the paper to move from the question:

Do manufacturing industries respond differently to monetary shocks?

to the more important question:

Can observable differences across industries explain why their monetary transmission profiles differ?

Research question

Why do Pakistan’s manufacturing industries respond differently to common monetary and macroeconomic shocks, and to what extent can these differences be explained by observable industry characteristics?

Theoretical Framework

The framework builds outward from a canonical three-equation New Keynesian core consisting of an intertemporal IS curve, a Phillips curve, and a Taylor-type monetary policy rule. This structure is augmented with a Bernanke-Gertler-Gilchrist financial accelerator, in which tighter monetary conditions can raise firms’ external financing costs as their balance-sheet positions weaken.

To move beyond the representative-firm assumption, industries are allowed to differ in their production structures. Each industry is characterized by a production technology combining domestic capital and labour with imported intermediate inputs. This allows capital intensity and dependence on imported inputs to vary across industries and provides a basis for understanding differences in financing costs and exchange-rate exposure.

The demand side is further differentiated according to product characteristics. In particular, the framework allows durable and non-essential goods to exhibit different intertemporal demand responses from essential, non-durable goods. This provides a theoretical channel through which monetary tightening can generate different demand effects across industries.

The framework also incorporates export demand and input-output linkages. Export exposure allows exchange-rate movements to affect industries through external competitiveness and foreign demand, while imported-input dependence introduces a production-cost channel. Input-output linkages allow monetary and macroeconomic shocks to propagate indirectly across industries through changes in downstream demand and production activity.

Taken together, these mechanisms provide a theoretical basis for heterogeneous monetary transmission across industries. The empirical analysis therefore examines whether observable differences in capital intensity, product nature, external orientation, and industrial linkages are systematically associated with the heterogeneous transmission profiles observed in the industry-level VECM results.

Empirical strategy

The study examines 14 large-scale manufacturing industries using a common six-variable VECM specification.

Each industry is modelled using:

Industry output, WPI, exchange rate, money market rate, government bond rate, and manufacturing credit.

The common specification is maintained across industries to preserve comparability while allowing the estimated long-run relationships and short-run adjustment dynamics to differ.

Structural-break-aware time-series procedures are used to account for major changes in the underlying data-generating process. Seasonal effects and relevant intervention and structural-break controls are incorporated into the empirical specification.

The VECM framework allows the study to distinguish between long-run equilibrium relationships and short-run adjustment dynamics.

Dynamic transmission analysis

The study uses orthogonalized impulse response functions to examine the dynamic response of industry output to innovations in:

WPI, exchange rate, money market rate, government bond rate, and manufacturing credit.

The analysis focuses on the direction, persistence, and evolution of industry responses rather than interpreting individual coefficients as standalone causal effects.

Forecast error variance decomposition is also used to examine the relative contribution of different innovations to output uncertainty over the 24-month horizon.

Together, these measures provide information on both the dynamic path of transmission and the relative importance of different innovations across industries.

Contribution to the literature

The paper will make three related contributions.

First, it will provide a systematic framework for explaining heterogeneous monetary transmission across manufacturing industries, rather than treating industrial heterogeneity as a descriptive finding.

Second, it will introduce an ex ante classification approach, where industry characteristics are defined independently of the estimated monetary responses. This reduces the risk of explaining an observed response simply by assigning an industry to a category after seeing the result.

Third, it will combine this classification framework with a common VECM-based empirical structure across industries, allowing differences in long-run relationships, adjustment dynamics, impulse responses, and shock contributions to be compared within a consistent modelling environment.

The resulting analysis will link observable characteristics of industries to the mechanisms through which monetary and macroeconomic shocks are transmitted to manufacturing output.

Current status

The theoretical framework has been developed. The empirical analysis is being implemented across the 14-industry sample using the common VECM framework, followed by robustness analysis and cross-industry comparison. No final comparative results are reported on this page at this stage.
